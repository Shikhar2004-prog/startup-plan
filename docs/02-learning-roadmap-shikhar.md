# Learning roadmap: Shikhar

What to learn, in what order, so you can make strong video content and grow the startup at the same time. Written 10 November 2026.

## The one rule: learn, then teach it within a week

Don't study for months before recording. Pick a topic, learn it, make a video on it within 7 days, and use that video in the course. Teaching is the fastest way to learn, and every hour of study becomes content.

## Step 1 (weeks 1 to 4): learn to teach on camera

This matters more than any new AI topic, because your research knowledge is already ahead of your students.

- **Explaining clearly.** Write a short script for every video: hook (why this matters), 3 key points, one demo, one takeaway. Practise explaining a topic to a friend in 2 minutes.
- **Recording.** OBS Studio (free) for screen and face, a basic lapel or USB mic (sound matters more than camera), a window or a cheap light in front of you.
- **Editing.** DaVinci Resolve (free) or CapCut. Learn cuts, zoom-ins on code, captions and simple thumbnails.
- **Slides and code on screen.** Large fonts, one idea per slide, Colab or VS Code zoomed in.
- **Target:** 4 published videos by the end of week 4. Quality will improve with every one.

## Step 2 (weeks 2 to 8): deepen what you will teach

Go in this order. It follows the Research Track, so each item becomes course material.

1. **How research works.** "How to Read a Paper" by S. Keshav (a short, classic guide), how to choose a research question, baselines, ablations and honest evaluation.
2. **ML and deep learning fundamentals, taught well.** Watch how good teachers explain, not just what: Andrej Karpathy's "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero", fast.ai's "Practical Deep Learning for Coders", and the free book "Dive into Deep Learning" (d2l.ai).
3. **Your speciality: vision and document AI.** Stanford CS231n lectures for vision basics; then OCR tools: Tesseract, PaddleOCR, TrOCR, Donut, LayoutLM-style models. Build one small end-to-end demo for each.
4. **Hugging Face.** The free Hugging Face courses (Transformers, and the LLM course). Students expect Hugging Face skills.
5. **GenAI and LLMs.** Prompting, RAG and simple agents, because students ask for it. Link it to your area, for example "LLMs for reading documents".
6. **Writing and presenting research.** Overleaf/LaTeX, making clean figures with matplotlib, and turning a paper into a 5-minute talk.

**Tip for GATE:** the maths behind ML (linear algebra, probability, statistics, optimisation) is also on the GATE syllabus, and GATE has a separate Data Science and AI paper (DA). Make some videos on these topics and you study for GATE and build content at the same time.

## Step 3 (start in week 1, keep going): startup skills

These are what most technical founders skip.

- **Talking to students before building.** Read "The Mom Test" by Rob Fitzpatrick (short). Then talk to 20 students about their problems with research and internships before launching the pilot.
- **Startup basics.** Y Combinator's free Startup School videos: finding users, pricing, doing things that don't scale.
- **Writing that sells.** Course pages, LinkedIn posts and video titles. Lead with the student's outcome, not the topic.
- **Personal brand.** Post weekly on LinkedIn about your research, your students' results and what you are learning. This is your cheapest marketing.
- **Numbers.** A simple sheet tracking signups, payments, completion and student results every week.

## How to focus

- **One track, one audience, one channel for 90 days.** Document AI Research Track, 2nd to 4th year students, YouTube. Say no to everything else until the pilot cohort finishes.
- **Fixed weekly time.** For example: exam prep in the mornings, learning and scripting on weekday evenings, recording and editing in one weekend block.
- **Weekly review (Sunday, 30 minutes).** What did I publish? What did students say? What do I drop next week?

## What you and Sanhita should both improve

- **Mentoring at scale:** giving feedback that students can act on, in a fixed format, so TAs can copy it later.
- **Asking for feedback:** after every webinar and class, a 3-question form.
- **Showing results:** collect every student win (report finished, internship interview, offer) with permission. This is the proof that sells the next cohort.
